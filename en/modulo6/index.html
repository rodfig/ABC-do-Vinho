History of the Bar

The word "bar" has a controversial origin. One of the most accepted versions argues that it comes from English bar, which means bar — referring to the physical structure that separates the customer from the attendant, especially in pubs British. Another explanation, also of English origin, says that the name refers to the lower bar of the counters, where customers rest their feet. There are still those who associate the term to the oak bar where the cowboys tied the horses to the saloons of the North American Old West.

Cocktail History

The origin of cocktail (or cocktail) dates back to the late 18th century. It is believed to have emerged in the United States during the independence period, although the exact circumstances remain mysterious. One of the best-known stories relates that, in 1779, an Irishman named Flanagan opened a tavern in Georgetown, Virginia. He and his daughter Betsy served mixed drinks called "Bracers", that is, stimulating and invigorating drinks.

At a special dinner, Flanagan and Betsy sacrificed their neighbor's roosters to serve as the main meal. After dinner, the famous Bracers were served in glasses decorated with multicolored feathers from slaughtered roosters. The look impressed the guests, who started calling the drinks "Cocktails" (rooster tails). Since then, the name has stuck.

IBA and Associação Barmen de Portugal

Founded on February 24, 1951, in Torquay, United Kingdom, by representatives from seven founding countries (Denmark, France, Italy, the Netherlands, Sweden, Switzerland and the United Kingdom), IBA is today the main global entity in the cocktail sector. Its first president was Bill Tarling, from the United Kingdom, followed by George Baker, George Siever (Switzerland), Pietro Grandi (Italy) and Kurt Sorensen (Denmark). Between 1963 and 1976, Angelo Zola, from Italy, presided over the association for 14 years and participated in the creation of IBA.

IBA's Mission: Connecting, educating and inspiring bartenders around the world.

Vision: Raise the standards and knowledge of professionals in the field at an international level.

Core values: Passion — Unity — Legacy

Currently, IBA has 67 member countries and 8 observer countries. More information is available on the official website: IBA-world.com

Competitions: The IBA 2024 World Cocktail Championship will be held between October 31st and November 3rd on the island of Madeira, in Portugal, organized by Associação Barmen de Portugal. The event will bring together bartenders from the 67 member countries of IBA, with tastings of classic cocktails and flair bartending.

Associação Barmen de Portugal (ABP) — Founded in 1970, still as Club Barmen de Portugal, ABP is a member of IBA and has played an important role in enhancing the bartending profession in the country. The association actively participates in international competitions, with achievements such as world championships, runner-up and third places, both in classical and in flair.

Barware

For correct bar service, it is essential to know and use the appropriate utensils:

  • Glass Mixtures: ideal for stirring delicate cocktails.
  • Classic Shaker: to mix ingredients with ice vigorously.
  • Boston Shaker: two pieces (one glass and one metal), used by more experienced bartenders.
  • Bar Spoon: long and spiral, to mix and create layers.
  • Jigger: beverage dispenser, essential for accurate measurements.
  • Strainer (Hawthorne strainer): used to strain the mixture when serving.
  • Fine Strainer: filters small particles of ice or fruit.
  • Tweezers: to handle ingredients hygienically.
  • Pylon: for macerating fruits, herbs and sugar.

Glasses — types and uses

THE RIGHT TYPE OF Glass FOR EVERY DRINK

1. Glass Highball/Collins — Highball and Collins glasses are very similar, and can often be substituted for one another. They are generally used for long drinks with plenty of ice. Use for: Whiskey Highball, Bloody Mary, Mojito.

2. Old Fashioned/On the rocks (Whisky Glass) — They are short glasses, often called lowball, on the rocks, or simply "whiskey glasses". They are generally used for small cocktails served over ice (on the rocks) or pure spirits, such as whiskey. Use for: Old Fashioned, Whisky Sour, Negroni.

3. Cocktail Glass (Martini Glass) — Very associated with Martinis, they are the best option for "neat" cocktails, without ice. The best way to store them is upright or on glasses hooks, and they can also be stored cold. Use for: Dry Martini, Cosmopolitan, Daiquiri.

4. Glass Coupe (or Coupette) — With a wide, shallow bowl on a rod, the glasses coupe They were originally used to serve champagne, but nowadays they are also used for cocktails in the Martini family. Use for: Espresso Martini, Sidecar, Manhattan.

5. Dose (Shot) — These are the smallest glasses behind the counter, used for serving pure drinks or small mixed drinks. Use for: Tequila, Boilermaker.

6. Glass of Cognac (Brandy) — Also called sniffer, they have this size and shape so that you can appreciate the color, the "tears" (streams of liquid that run down the glass) and the aromas of the drink. It is typically used for dark spirits and simple brandy cocktails. Use for: Cognac, B&B.

7. Glass de Champagne (Glass Flute) — A glass with a thin stem and a cone-shaped bowl, created to retain bubbles for longer. Typically used to serve champagne and cocktails based on this drink. Use for: Kir Royale, Bellini, Mimosa.

8. Glass from white wine — white wine glasses tend to be wider and taller, and the best way to store them is chilled. Typically used for white wine or cocktails of this drink. Use for: white wine, Sangria, Wine Spritzer.

9. Glass from red wine — The red wine glasses are wide and rounded, to release the flavor and aromas of the wine. The wide bowl also enhances the aroma of gin botanicals, with space for creative mixes and decoration ingredients. Use for: red wine, Gin Tonica.

10. Glasses and Beer Mugs — There is a wide variety of styles and shapes, and many are made of tempered glass, which maintains the temperature and foam better. Use for: Beer, Ciders and Ales.

Bar Mise-en-place

Before serving, the bartender must ensure:

  • Clean and organized bar;
  • Glasses in sufficient quantity and checked for hygiene;
  • Clean and ready serving utensils;
  • Bottles tidy, accessible and checked;
  • Sustainable bases and straws to accompany drinks;
  • Clean and available serving tray.

Customer Service

Service is an art and it starts with:

  • Reception with a smile at the entrance;
  • Greeting by name (when possible);
  • Accommodate the customer, offer the letter and be available for clarification;
  • Confirm whether the customer wants variations in the chosen cocktail;
  • Repeat the order to avoid errors;
  • Carry out upselling and cross-selling, justifying the suggestions;
  • Always serve with kindness, using a glass base;
  • Keep tables clean, intervening whenever necessary;
  • Correctly measure all doses with the jigger;
  • Taste the drink with a small sustainable straw, if necessary.

Golden Rules of the Bar

  • Avoid mixing strong drinks such as whiskey with cognac, or vodka and rum, in the same cocktail.
  • During prolonged aperitifs, avoid alternating cocktails with different alcoholic bases.
  • A good cocktail generally has three to five elements: an alcoholic base, one or two liqueurs, juice or cream and, occasionally, a syrup.
  • Use natural juices whenever possible. When not, opt for brands with quality control.
  • Utensils and glasses must be clean. A dirty glass or faded decoration disqualifies any cocktail.
  • Ingredients must be fresh and of good quality — the garnish fruit, especially.
  • Most cocktails should be served in a chilled glass.
  • The decor should complement the style of the drink. Never exaggerate the size or volume.
  • Sliced fruit should be prepared in advance and refrigerated.
  • Never overfill the glass — it could overflow and compromise the presentation.
  • In cocktails with soft drinks or carbonated water, these ingredients should be added at the end, never at the end. shaker or blender.
  • The cocktail, once ready, must be served immediately.

Types of Drinks

Drinks can be classified into:

  • Simple: a single drink (e.g. straight whiskey);
  • Compounds: mixing two or more drinks;
  • Alcohol-free: juices, waters, soft drinks;
  • With alcohol: wines, spirits, cocktails.

Specific Categories:

  • Short Drinks: 60 to 90 ml, generally served in cocktail glasses or glasses old-fashioned.
  • Long Drinks: prepared with distillates, liqueurs, bitters, etc., served in glasses longdrink called Highballs. Complete with fruit juices, carbonated water, soft drinks and plenty of ice. Average volume of 150 ml to 300 ml.
  • Hot Drinks: made with coffee, chocolate or hot water. Served in winter in mugs.
  • Frozen Drinks: blended in a blender, with distilled drinks, liqueurs, ice, ice cream, cookies or condensed milk. Creamy and smooth texture.
  • Appetite stimulants: acidic or bitter taste, served before meals.
  • Digestives: help the metabolism of food, served after meals.
  • Nutritious: complete the body's caloric values.
  • Refreshing: served outside of meals, with lots of ice in glass Long Drink.
  • Physical stimulants: hot cocktails for the cold.
  • Cobblers: prepared with Wine, Brandy or Whiskey, fruit, sugar and ice.
  • Coolers: cider-based preparations, Ginger Ale or other soft drink.
  • Crusted: They are based on a distillate, lemon juice, Curaçao, sugar and ice, served in cocktail glasses with a crusted rim.

Alexander

The Alexander is a creamy and elegant cocktail that combines the robustness of cognac with the sweetness of cocoa liqueur and the smoothness of fresh cream.

Ingredients

  • 30 ml of Cognac
  • 30 ml Crème de Cacao (dark)
  • 30 ml of Fresh Cream

Preparation Method

  1. Pour all ingredients into a shaker filled with ice cubes.
  2. Shake vigorously and strain into a chilled cocktail glass.

Trim

Sprinkle fresh ground nutmeg on top.

History

The Alexander cocktail was born in London in 1922, created by Henry McElhone at Ciro's Club in honor of a famous bride. Initially called Panama, it used gin instead of brandy and light cream of cocoa instead of dark.

Variations

  • Grasshopper: Use green crème de menthe instead of cognac.
  • Alexandra: Use light cocoa cream instead of dark and substitute nutmeg for cocoa powder.
  • Alexander's Sister: Use crème de menthe instead of crème de cacao.
  • Alejandro: Replace the brandy with rum.

Americano

Americano is a classic Italian aperitif that combines the bitterness of Campari with the sweetness of vermouth and the freshness of sparkling water.

Ingredients

  • 30 ml Campari Bitter
  • 30 ml of Sweet Red Vermouth
  • A splash of sparkling water

Preparation Method

  1. Mix the ingredients directly in a glass Old Fashioned filled with ice cubes.
  2. Add a splash of sparkling water and stir gently.

Trim

Garnish with half an orange slice and lemon peel.

History

The history of the Americano dates back to the second half of the 19th century in Gaspare Campari's bar in Milan. The cocktail gained notoriety through tributes from the cinematic world, including James Bond (007 Casino Royale), whose favorite cocktail was precisely the Americano. The nickname "Americano" came about when Primo Carnera, a giant Italian boxer, became world heavyweight champion at Madison Square Garden in New York. When Carnera returned to Italy with the title, he was welcomed with this very Italian cocktail which, for the occasion, was called "Americano". The Americano entered the IBA Cocktail List only in 1987, but in reality it was a cocktail that spread throughout Italy during the 1930s.

Daiquiri

Daiquiri is a refreshing and balanced cocktail that exemplifies the perfect harmony between rum, lime and sugar.

Ingredients

  • 60 ml of White Cuban Rum
  • 20 ml of Fresh Lime Juice
  • 2 bar spoons of Refined Sugar

Preparation Method

  1. In a cocktail shaker, add all the ingredients. Stir well to dissolve the sugar.
  2. Add ice and shake. Strain into a chilled cocktail glass.

Trim

Not applicable.

History

The origins of the creation of Daiquiri are somewhat controversial. Some believe the cocktail was created in 1896 by an American engineer, Jennings Cox, who was living in Cuba at the time and overseeing mines near the town of Daiquiri. It is thought he created the famous cocktail after running out of gin during a cocktail party. Improvising with the ingredients he had on hand — rum, lime and sugar — this became the basis of the drink we know today. Daiquiri really came to life in a small bar in Havana when bartender Constantino Ribalaigua Vert added his own twist and used shaved ice to create Daiquiri Frozen. Around this time, Ernest Hemingway, who lived in Cuba, tried the delicious cocktail, fell in love with this epic concoction and became a regular customer.

Variations

  • Daiquiri Frozen (with crushed ice)
  • Daiquiri on the rocks (served with ice)
  • Strawberry Daiquiri (with strawberries)

Dry Martini

Dry Martini is considered the king of cocktails, an elegant and sophisticated drink that represents the essence of classic cocktails.

Ingredients

  • 60 ml of Gin
  • 10 ml of Dry Vermouth

Preparation Method

  1. Pour all ingredients into a mixing glass with ice cubes. Stir well.
  2. Strain into a chilled martini glass.

Trim

Squeeze oil from the lemon peel over the drink, or garnish with green olives if requested.

History

One of the most frequently cited theories is that "Professor" Jerry Thomas, a famous and influential 19th-century bartender, invented the drink at the Hotel Occidental in San Francisco, sometime in the late 1850s or early 1860s. According to the story, a miner, about to leave on a trip to Martinez, California, placed a gold nugget on the bar and asked Thomas to prepare him something special. Thomas produced a drink containing Old Tom gin (sweetened), vermouth, bitters and Maraschino, and nicknamed it "Martinez" in honor of the customer's fate.

Variations

  • Dirty Martini (with olive brine)
  • Vodka Martini (with vodka instead of gin)
  • Gibson (with cocktail onion)
  • Some versions are prepared in a cocktail shaker instead of a mixing glass.

Gin Fizz

Gin Fizz is a refreshing and effervescent cocktail that combines the botanicality of gin with the acidity of lemon and the effervescence of sparkling water.

Ingredients

  • 45 ml of Gin
  • 30 ml of Fresh Lemon Juice
  • 10 ml Simple Syrup
  • Sparkling Water Splash

Preparation Method

  1. Shake all ingredients with ice except sparkling water.
  2. Pour into a tall glass and Fino, finish with a splash of sparkling water.

Note: Serve without ice.

Trim

Garnish with a lemon wedge and, optionally, lemon peel.

History

Around 1750, Gin Fizz was born, a cocktail that would become one of the most popular drinks in the world. At that time, sailors were most affected by vitamin C deficiency and scurvy, a potentially fatal disease. Admiral Nelson came up with the idea of ​​mixing gin and lemon to help prevent the disease among his men.

John / Tom Collins

The Collins is a refreshing summer cocktail that combines gin, lemon juice and sparkling water in a tall glass.

Ingredients

  • 45 ml of Gin
  • 30 ml of Fresh Lemon Juice
  • 15 ml Simple Syrup
  • 60 ml of Sparkling Water

Preparation Method

  1. Pour all ingredients directly into a glass highball filled with ice.
  2. Stir gently.

Trim

Garnish with a lemon wedge and maraschino cherry.

History

It appears that the Collins evolved from Gin Punch, a popular drink of the time, and its creation and name are attributed to John (or possibly Jim) Collins, head waiter at Limmer's Hotel on Conduit Street in London during the late 19th century.

Variations

  • Use 'Old Tom' gin for the Tom Collins.

Manhattan

Manhattan is a sophisticated and balanced cocktail that represents the elegance of classic American cocktails.

Ingredients

  • 50 ml of Rye Whiskey
  • 20 ml of Sweet Red Vermouth
  • 1 dash of Angostura Bitters

Preparation Method

  1. Pour all ingredients into a mixing glass with ice cubes. Stir well.
  2. Strain into a chilled cocktail glass.

Trim

Garnish with a cocktail cherry.

History

Popular history suggests that the drink originated at the Manhattan Club in New York in the mid-1870s, where it was invented by Iain Marshall for a banquet hosted by Jennie Jerome (Lady Randolph Churchill, Winston's mother) in honor of presidential candidate Samuel J. Tilden.

Variations

  • There are numerous variations of Manhattan, including versions with different types of whiskey and vermouth.

Negroni

Negroni is an iconic Italian aperitif that offers a perfect balance between bitter, sweet and herbal.

Ingredients

  • 30 ml of Gin
  • 30 ml Campari Bitter
  • 30 ml of Sweet Red Vermouth

Preparation Method

  1. Pour all ingredients directly into a chilled glass Old Fashioned filled with ice.
  2. Stir gently.

Trim

Decorate with half an orange slice.

History

1919, The Beginning: The story of Negroni begins at Caffè Casoni in Florence. There is no documented historical record, but it is believed by cocktail experts that Count Camillo Negroni invented the drink when he ordered an Americano made with gin instead of the usual sparkling water.

Old Fashioned

Old Fashioned is considered the ancestor of all cocktails, representing the original cocktail formula in its purest form.

Ingredients

  • 45 ml Bourbon or Rye Whiskey
  • 1 Lump of Sugar
  • A few dashes of Angostura Bitters
  • A few drops of water

Preparation Method

  1. Place the lump of sugar in the glass Old Fashioned and saturate it with bitters, add a few drops of water. Crush until dissolved.
  2. Fill the glass with ice cubes and add the whiskey. Stir gently.

Trim

Garnish with an orange slice or peel, and a cocktail cherry.

History

Old Fashioned was first created in the 1800s, harkening back to the early days of cocktails. Although, at the time, it was known simply as a Whiskey Cocktail, a drink that followed the basic cocktail formula that included a spirit, sugar, water and bitters.

Sidecar

The Side Car is an elegant and balanced cocktail that combines the richness of cognac with the citrus acidity and sweetness of orange liqueur.

Ingredients

  • 50 ml of Cognac
  • 20 ml of Triple Sec
  • 20 ml of Fresh Lemon Juice

Preparation Method

  1. Pour all ingredients into a shaker, shake well with ice.
  2. Strain into a chilled cocktail glass.

Trim

Not applicable.

History

Although it is not 100% confirmed, it is speculated that it was invented at the end of the First World War, somewhere in London or Paris. David A. Embury wrote in his book "Fine Art of Mixing Drink" in 1948 that the drink was created by a friend of his. He also notes that the cocktail got its name from the Sidecar on the motorcycle.

Whiskey/Pisco Sour

The Whiskey Sour is a classic cocktail that exemplifies the perfect balance of sweet and sour, with the option to add egg white for texture.

Ingredients

  • 45 ml of Bourbon Whiskey
  • 25 ml of Fresh Lemon Juice
  • 20 ml Sugar Syrup
  • 20 ml Egg White (Optional)

Preparation Method

  1. Pour all ingredients into a cocktail shaker filled with ice. Shake well.
  2. Strain into a glass cobbler. If served "On the rocks", strain the ingredients into a glass Old Fashioned filled with ice.

Note: If using egg whites, shake a little harder to release and incorporate the white foam.

Trim

Garnish with half an orange slice and maraschino cherry, optionally use orange peel.

History

The Whiskey Sour officially dates back to the 1860s, but sailors in the British Navy were drinking something very similar long before that. On long sea voyages, water was not always reliable, so to combat this, distillates were often used.

Variations

  • Pisco Sour (substitute whiskey for pisco)

Bellini

The Bellini is an elegant and refreshing cocktail that captures the essence of ripe peaches in an effervescent drink.

Ingredients

  • 100 ml of Prosecco
  • 50 ml of White Peach Puree

Preparation Method

  1. Pour the peach puree into the mixing glass with ice, add the Prosecco wine.
  2. Stir gently and pour in into a chilled flute.

Trim

Not applicable.

History

The Bellini was invented sometime between 1934 and 1948 by Giuseppe Cipriani, founder of Harry's Bar in Venice, Italy. He named the drink Bellini because its unique pink color reminded him of a saint's toga in a painting by 15th-century Venetian artist Giovanni Bellini.

Variations

  • PUCCINI: Fresh Tangerine Juice
  • ROSSINI: Fresh Strawberry Puree
  • TINTORETTO: Fresh Pomegranate Juice

Black Russian

The Black Russian is a simple yet sophisticated cocktail that combines the neutrality of vodka with the richness of coffee liqueur.

Ingredients

  • 50 ml of Vodka
  • 20 ml of Coffee Liqueur

Preparation Method

  1. Pour the ingredients into the glass Old Fashioned filled with ice cubes.
  2. Stir gently.

Trim

Not applicable.

History

The Black Russian cocktail first appeared in 1949 and is attributed to Gustave Tops, a Belgian bartender, who created it at the Hotel Metropole in Brussels in honor of Perle Mesta, then US Ambassador to Luxembourg.

Variations

  • WHITE RUSSIAN: Add fresh cream on top and stir slowly.

Bloody Mary

The Bloody Mary is a complex and flavorful cocktail, often consumed as a morning drink due to its supposedly healing qualities.

Ingredients

  • 45 ml of Vodka
  • 90 ml of Tomato Juice
  • 15 ml of Fresh Lemon Juice
  • 2 dashes of Worcestershire Sauce
  • Tabasco, Celery Salt, Pepper (To taste)

Preparation Method

  1. Gently stir all ingredients in a mixing glass with ice.
  2. Pour into glass rocks.

Note: If requested served with ice, pour into glass highball.

Trim

Celery, Lemon Slice (Optional)

History

The creation of the Bloody Mary is often credited to Fernand Petiot in the 1920s while a young bartender at Harry's New York Bar in Paris. However, it appears that he simply spiked an existing and well-established combination of vodka and tomato juice while working at the St. Regis Hotel, New York during the 1940s.

Variations

  • Virgin Mary (without vodka)

Caipirinha

Caipirinha is Brazil's national cocktail, a refreshing and vibrant drink that celebrates Brazilian cachaça.

Ingredients

  • 60 ml of Cachaça
  • 1 lime cut into small pieces
  • 4 teaspoons of White Cane Sugar

Preparation Method

  1. Place the lime and sugar in a double glass Old Fashioned and crush gently.
  2. Fill the glass with broken ice and add the cachaça. Stir gently to combine the ingredients.

Trim

Not applicable.

History

Although the origin of the drink is unknown, there is consensus in the academic community that Caipirinha was invented in the interior of São Paulo, Brazil, in 1918.

Variations

  • CAIPIROSKA: Instead of cachaça, use vodka
  • CAIPIRISSIMA: Use rum
  • CAIPIRÃO: Use Licor Beirão

Champagne Cocktail

The Champagne Cocktail is an elegant and festive drink that elevates champagne with aromatic touches of bitters and cognac.

Ingredients

  • 90 ml of Chilled Champagne
  • 10 ml of Cognac
  • 2 pinches of Angostura Bitters
  • A few drops of Grand Marnier (optional)
  • 1 lump of sugar

Preparation Method

  1. Place the lump of sugar with 2 pinches of bitters in a large champagne glass, add the brandy.
  2. Gently pour the chilled champagne.

Trim

Garnish with orange zest and maraschino cherry.

History

The first known written mention of the champagne cocktail can be found in "The Bon Vivant's Companion" by Jerry Thomas, which was published in 1862. In this influential cocktail book, Thomas included a recipe for the champagne cocktail, describing it as a mixture of champagne, sugar and aromatic bitters.

Cosmopolitan

Cosmopolitan is a modern and elegant cocktail that has become iconic through popular culture.

Ingredients

  • 40 ml of Citron Vodka
  • 15 ml of Cointreau
  • 15 ml of Fresh Lime Juice
  • 30 ml of Cranberry Juice

Preparation Method

  1. Add all ingredients to a cocktail shaker filled with ice. Shake well.
  2. Strain into a large cocktail glass.

Trim

Decorate with a lemon spiral.

History

The origin of Cosmopolitan is disputed, with some stories tracing it from the gay community of Provincetown in the 1970s, moving west to Cleveland and Minneapolis, and reaching San Francisco. From there it moved back east, with the contemporary recipe being mixed in 1989 in New York City. He became known in the series "Sex and the City", drunk by Carrie Bradshaw (Sarah Jessica Parker).

Cuba Libre

The Cuba Libre is a simple yet historic cocktail that combines rum, cola and lime into a refreshing drink.

Ingredients

  • 50 ml of White Rum
  • 120 ml of Coca-Cola
  • 10 ml of Fresh Lime Juice

Preparation Method

  1. Build all ingredients in a glass highball filled with ice.

Trim

Decorate with lime slice.

History

As the story goes, in the year 1900, an American Army captain stationed in Havana during the Spanish-American War poured Coca-Cola and a squeeze of lime into his Bacardí and toasted his Cuban comrades by shouting across the bar: "For Cuba Libre!" ("For a free Cuba!"). And so, a legend was born.

Margarita

Margarita is an iconic Mexican cocktail that celebrates tequila with the perfect acidity of lime and the sweetness of orange liqueur.

Ingredients

  • 50 ml 100% Agave Tequila
  • 20 ml of Triple Sec
  • 15 ml Freshly Squeezed Lime Juice

Preparation Method

  1. Add all ingredients to a cocktail shaker with ice. Shake.
  2. Strain into a chilled cocktail glass.

Trim

Half a rim of salt (optional).

History

Francisco "Pancho" Morales says he created Margarita in the summer of 1942 while working at a bar called Tommy's Place on Avenida Juarez in Ciudad Juarez, Mexico, after "A lady came in and said, 'Let me have a Magnolia'" on July 4, 1942.

Variations

  • Frozen Margarita (with crushed ice)

Mint Julep

The Mint Julep is a classic American Southern cocktail that celebrates fresh mint and bourbon.

Ingredients

  • 60 ml of Bourbon Whiskey
  • 4 sprigs of fresh mint
  • 1 teaspoon of Powdered Sugar
  • 2 teaspoons of Water

Preparation Method

  1. In a stainless steel Julep glass, gently crush the mint with sugar and water.
  2. Fill the glass with broken ice, add the bourbon and stir well until the glass freezes.

Trim

Decorate with a sprig of mint.

History

The mint julep originated in the southern United States, probably during the 18th century. The earliest known mentions date back to 1770 and include a satirical play by Robert Munford, "The Candidate". The Mint Julep is the drink of the Kentucky Derby. "The first written mention of a julep glass being awarded as a horse racing trophy [was] in 1816. This is proof that juleps were highly valued and associated with horse racing."

Mojito

Mojito is a refreshing Cuban cocktail that combines rum, mint, lime and sparkling water in perfect harmony.

Ingredients

  • 45 ml of White Cuban Rum
  • 20 ml of Fresh Lime Juice
  • 6 sprigs of mint
  • 2 teaspoons of White Cane Sugar
  • Sparkling Water

Preparation Method

  1. Mix the mint sprigs with sugar and lime juice.
  2. Add a splash of sparkling water and fill the glass with ice.
  3. Pour in the rum and finish with sparkling water. Stir lightly to coat all the ingredients.

Trim

Decorate with mint sprigs and lime slice.

History

The original Mojito is said to have been a medicinal drink to combat disease on the island of Cuba. A rum brandy-like alcohol was mixed with mint, lime and sugar cane syrup to ward off disease.

Moscow Mule

The Moscow Mule is an American cocktail traditionally served in a copper mug that enhances the spicy flavor of ginger beer.

Ingredients

  • 45 ml of Smirnoff Vodka
  • 120 ml of Ginger Beer
  • 10 ml of fresh lime juice

Preparation Method

  1. In a Mule mug or glass rocks, combine the vodka and ginger beer.
  2. Add the lime juice and stir gently to combine all the ingredients.

Trim

Garnish with a slice of lime.

History

The Moscow Mule was born on that day in 1941. The perfect combination of vodka and ginger beer, housed in a solid copper mug that kept the drink cold and enhanced its flavor and aroma, resulting in a cocktail that America would fall in love with for the next century and beyond.

Grasshopper

Grasshopper is a creamy and refreshing cocktail with a mint and chocolate flavor, perfect as a digestive.

Ingredients

  • 20 ml of Crème de Cacao (White)
  • 20 ml of Crème de Menthe (Green)
  • 20 ml of Fresh Cream

Preparation Method

  1. Pour all ingredients into a cocktail shaker filled with ice. Shake vigorously for a few seconds.
  2. Strain into a chilled cocktail glass.

Trim

History

Philibert Guichet invented the drink that would become known as Grasshopper, for a 1918 cocktail competition in New York City. Guichet's cocktail took second place and became so popular back home in New Orleans that it has maintained a permanent spot on Tujague's cocktail menu ever since.

ASI Assessment Criteria

The Association of Sommellerie International (ASI) bartending competition evaluates candidates based on the following key points:

Technical Aspects

The Sommelier must demonstrate technical precision by using the correct ingredients and measuring them accurately. It is essential to prepare the cocktail using the correct method, whether in a cocktail shaker, mixing glass or directly in the serving glass, always using the appropriate utensils for each technique.

Presentation and Finish

Preparing and using the correct garnish is key, as is ensuring the cocktail's appearance is perfect. The professional must taste the cocktail to ensure the balance of flavors before serving it to the customer, always presenting the drink on a suitable base.

Organization and Professionalism

The tidiness of the workstation must be maintained throughout the process, demonstrating organization and efficiency. It is crucial to demonstrate bartending skills with confidence and professional aplomb.

Communication and Service

During preparation, the Sommelier must communicate and describe the recipe and method, being prepared to answer the examiner's questions. It is important to ask if the customer would like a food recommendation, providing a detailed description and explaining the pairing of the choice with the cocktail served.

Knowledge and Pairing

The professional must demonstrate in-depth knowledge of each cocktail, including its history, variations and organoleptic characteristics. The ability to suggest appropriate Pairings with food is essential for full service.

This guide represents the fundamental basis for any bar professional who wishes to master the art of classic cocktail making, combining technique, historical knowledge and service excellence.
